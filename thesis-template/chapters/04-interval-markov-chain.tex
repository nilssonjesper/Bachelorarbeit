We start by introducing concepts which we will use in the following sections.
\begin{definition}[Interval Markov Chain]\label{def:IMC}
    An interval discrete-time Markov chain (IDTMC) is a tuple
    $\IMC=(S^\IMC,\PFlower^\IMC,\PFupper^\IMC,\Initlower^\IMC,\Initupper^\IMC)$, where
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
        \item $S^\IMC$ is a countable, nonempty set of states,
        \item $\PFlower^\IMC: S^\IMC \times S^\IMC \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the lower bound on the transition probability function, such that $\PFlower^\IMC(s_i,s_j) \leq \PF(s_i,s_j)$ for all $s_i,s_j\in S^\IMC$,
        \item $\PFupper^\IMC: S^\IMC \times S^\IMC \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the upper bound on the transition probability function, such that $\PF(s_i,s_j) \leq \PFupper^\IMC(s_i,s_j)$ for all $s_i,s_j\in S^\IMC$,
        \item $\Initlower^\IMC: S^\IMC \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the lower bound on the initial distribution, such that $\Initlower^\IMC(s) \leq \iota_{init}(s)$ for all $s\in S^\IMC$, and
        \item $\Initupper^\IMC: S \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the upper bound on the initial distribution, such that $\iota_{init}(s) \leq \Initupper^\IMC(s)$ for all $s\in S^\IMC$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Path of an IDTMC]
    Similarly to DTMCs, an \textit{infinite path} in an IDTMC $\IMC = (S,)$ is a sequence $\pi = (s_0,s_1,\dots)$ of states with $s_0,s_1,\dots \in S^\IMC$, such that $\PFlower(\pi[k],\pi[k+1])>0$ for all $k\in \NN$.
\end{definition}

\emph{\textbf{Fixed-initial-state definition.}}
In the literature, see, e.g., \cite{complexity_model_checking_interval-valued_DTMCs}, it is common to represent the initial distribution by an additional initial state rather than introducing a separate initial-distribution function. In this case, an IMC is given as a tuple $\IMC_{\mathrm{uni}} = (S,\PFlower,\PFupper,s_{\mathrm{in}}),$ where \(s_{\mathrm{in}}\) is the unique initial state. We refer to this as the \emph{fixed-initial-state definition}.

More precisely, let $\IMC = (S^\IMC,\PFlower^\IMC,\PFupper^\IMC,\Initlower^\IMC,\Initupper^\IMC)$ be an IMC in our definition, and let $\iota^\IMC_{\mathrm{init}}$ denote its initial distribution. The corresponding fixed-initial-state representation is obtained by introducing a fresh state \(s_{\mathrm{in}}\) and defining $S^{\IMC_{\mathrm{uni}}} = S^\IMC\cup\{s_{\mathrm{in}}\}.$

The lower and upper transition-probability functions are given by
\[
\PFlower^{\IMC_{\mathrm{uni}}}(s_i,s_j)
=
\begin{cases}
    \PFlower^\IMC(s_i,s_j),
        & s_i,s_j\neq s_{\mathrm{in}},\\
    \iota^\IMC_{\mathrm{init}}(s_j),
        & s_i=s_{\mathrm{in}},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
and
\[
\PFupper^{\IMC_{\mathrm{uni}}}(s_i,s_j)
=
\begin{cases}
    \PFupper^\IMC(s_i,s_j),
        & s_i,s_j\neq s_{\mathrm{in}},\\
    \iota^\IMC_{\mathrm{init}}(s_j),
        & s_i=s_{\mathrm{in}},\\
    0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The same construction applies to DTMCs. Let $\MC=(S^\MC,\PF^\MC,\iota^\MC_{\mathrm{init}})$ be a DTMC. Its corresponding fixed-initial-state representation is $\MC_{\mathrm{uni}} = (S^\MC\cup\{s_{\mathrm{in}}\},\PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}},s_{\mathrm{in}}),$ where
\[
    \PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}}(s_i,s_j)
    =
    \begin{cases}
        \PF^\MC(s_i,s_j),
            & s_i,s_j\neq s_{\mathrm{in}},\\
        \iota^\MC_{\mathrm{init}}(s_j),
            & s_i=s_{\mathrm{in}},\\
        0, & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\]
The two representations induce the same probability space up to the natural bijection between their paths. In particular, define
\[
    \phi: Paths_{\inf}^{\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}} \longrightarrow Paths_{\inf}^{\MC}, (s_{\mathrm{in}},s_0,s_1,\ldots) \mapsto (s_0,s_1,\ldots).
\]

\(\phi\) is a bijection, since \(s_{\mathrm{in}}\) has no incoming transitions, every infinite path of \(\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}\) starts with \(s_{\mathrm{in}}\).

To see that the probability measures correspond under \(\phi\), consider a finite path $\pi_{\mathrm{uni}} = (s_{\mathrm{in}},s_0,\ldots,s_n)$ of \(\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}\), and let $\pi=(s_0,\ldots,s_n)$ be the corresponding finite path of \(\MC\). Then
\begin{align}
Pr^{\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}}(\pi_{\mathrm{uni}}) 
&= \PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}}(s_{\mathrm{in}},s_0) \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{uni}}}(s_k,s_{k+1}) \notag
&= \iota^\MC_{\mathrm{init}}(s_0) \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \PF^\MC(s_k,s_{k+1}) \notag
&= Pr^\MC(\pi).
\end{align}
Thus, corresponding cylinder sets have the same probability, and consequently the probability measures on the two path spaces coincide under the bijection \(\phi\).

Hence, adding a unique initial state is merely a different representation of the initial distribution and does not change the probabilistic behaviour of the model. In particular, results formulated for the fixed-initial-state definition can be transferred to our definition by applying the above construction.

To resolve the uncertainty of the initial distribution and transition probability function, we need to define semantics to interpret the Interval Markov Chain. In other words, we need to resolve an Interval Markov Chain to some set of Markov Chains that correspond to it. We say that all of these DTMCs $\MC$ satisfy the IDTMC $\IMC$ and denote it as $\MC \models \IMC$. We assume in this thesis that there exists at least one satisfying DTMC for every IDTMC.

\begin{assumption}\label{assmp:finiteness_IDTMCs}
    In this thesis, we only consider finite IDTMCs, i.e., IDTMCs that have a finite state space.
    Moreover, we will only consider IDTMCs where there exists at least one satisfying DTMC $\MC \models \IMC$ for each state $s\in S^\IMC$, such that $s$ is reachable with a positive probability from an initial state in $\MC$. Because, if a given state was not reachable in any satisfying DTMC, then the EVT of this state would trivially be $0$ for all satisfying DTMCs and have no impact on the EVTs of other states.
\end{assumption}

In this thesis, we will consider two popular semantics: the \textit{once-and-for-all}, also known as the \textit{Uncertain Markov Chain (UMC) semantic}, and the \textit{IMDP semantic}.
We will first define the former, similar to \cite{Semantics_Quant-Analysis,Semantics_Model-Checking_omega-Regular,Semantics_LTL_Model_Checking}. To this end, we fix an IDTMC $\IMC = (S^\IMC,\PFlower,\PFupper,\Initlower,\Initupper)$ and a DTMC $\MC = (S^\MC,\PF,\iota_{init})$.

\begin{definition}[Once-and-for-all/UMC Semantic]\label{def:once-and-for-all_semantic}
    A DTMC $\MC$ satisfies an IDTMC $\IMC$ if and only if $S^\IMC = S^\MC$, and for all $s,s'\in S^\IMC$, $\PF^{\MC}(s,s')\in[\PFlower^{\IMC}(s,s'),\PFupper^{\IMC}(s,s')]$ and $\iota_{init}^{\MC}(s)\in[\Initlower^{\IMC}(s),\Initupper^{\IMC}(s)]$.
\end{definition}
In other words, any DTMC whose initial distribution and transition probabilities lie in the intervals defined by the IDTMC is satisfactory. Thus, we can interpret the \textit{UMC} semantic for an IDTMC, as the initial distribution and probability function being chosen from the intervals \textit{once-and-for-all}.
Moreover, notice that $\MC$ has the same cardinality as $\IMC$, i.e., $|S^\MC| = |S^\IMC|$.
If $\MC$ satisfies $\IMC$ under the UMC semantic, we denote this as $\MC\models_{\text{U}}\IMC$.

\begin{example}
    Consider the IDTMC $\IMC_E$ in \Cref{fig:IMC_for_semantic}. The DTMC $\MC_U = (S^{\IMC_E},\PF,\iota_{init})$ depicted in \Cref{fig:UMC_semantic} satisfies $\IMC$ under the UMC semantic, i.e., $\MC_U \models_U \IMC$, where the transition probability function is given by
    \[
        \PF = \begin{bmatrix}
            0 & 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & 0 & 0.8 & 0.2 \\
            0.8 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0.2 \\
            0 & 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1
        \end{bmatrix}\ ,
    \]
    and the initial distribution
    \[\iota_{init} = (1,0,0,0,0)\]
\end{example}

This choice of a probability function is made non-deterministically by an external environment.

In \cite{Badings2025} this external environment is defined as the \textit{nature}.
\begin{definition}[Nature]\label{def:nature}
    A \textit{nature} for an IDTMC $\IMC$ is a function $\eta: Paths^\IMC_{fin} \rightarrow Steps^\IMC$ that assigns each finite path to a valid transition probability function, i.e., $\eta(\pi)\in Steps^\IMC(last(\pi))$.
\end{definition}

Notice, that we only define this external environment for the transition probability function and not the initial distribution. Recall, that results for the \textit{fixed-initial-state definition} can be applied to our definition by applying the shown construction. Thus, it suffices to define this external environment for the transition probability function.

By applying a given \textit{nature} $\eta$ to $\IMC$, we resolve the uncertainty of the probability function, as $\eta$ specifies a probability function $\PF \in Steps^\IMC(s)$ for every finite path in $\IMC$.

If $\MC$ satisfies $\IMC$ under the IMDP semantic, we denote this as $\MC\models_{\text{I}}\IMC$.

An important type of nature, which we require in this thesis, is the \textit{stationary nature}. The following definition is based on \cite{Badings2025}.

\begin{definition}[Stationary Nature]
    A nature $\eta$ for an IDTMC $\IMC$ is \textit{stationary} if for all paths $\pi,\pi' \in Paths_{fin}^\IMC$, it holds that 
    \[ last(\pi) = last(\pi') \implies \eta(\pi) = \eta(\pi')\ .\]
\end{definition}
Intuitively, this implies that $\eta$ only depends on the current state of the model, i.e., the state the model is in. Thus, stationary natures are functions of the form 
\[\eta: S^\IMC \rightarrow Steps^\IMC \ .\]

Notice that the induced DTMCs of these stationary natures are finite in size.

\begin{definition}[DTMC of an IDTMC Induced by a Stationary Nature]\label{def:induced_DTMC_stat_Nature}
    For an IDTMC $\IMC$ and a stationary nature $\eta$ on $\IMC$, the induced Markov Chain $\MC_\eta$ is given by 
    \[ \MC_\eta = (S^\IMC, \PF^{\MC_\eta}, \iota^\IMC_{init})\ ,\]
    where for all $s_i\in S^\IMC$ and $\eta(s_i) = \PF$:
    \[ \PF^{\MC_\eta}(s_i,s_j) = \begin{cases*}
        \PF(s_i,s_j) & $s_j \in N(s_i)$,\\
        0 & otherwise.
    \end{cases*}
    \]
\end{definition}

\subsection{Relation between the semantics}\label{sec:relation_between_the_semantics}
Intuitively, the \textit{IMDP} semantic is more expressive than the \textit{UMC} semantic. More specifically for any given IDTMC $\IMC$, a DTMC $\MC$ that satisfies $\IMC$ according to the UMC semantic, i.e., $\MC \models_U \IMC$, does also satisfy $\IMC$ according to the IMDP semantic, i.e., $\MC \models_I \IMC$.
\begin{theorem}\label{th:relation_of_semantics}
    For any IDTMC $\IMC$, if $\MC \models_U \IMC$, then $\MC \models_I \IMC$.
\end{theorem}

We can prove this theorem using the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:relation_stat_nature_UMC}
    Given an IDTMC $\IMC$ and stationary nature $\eta$, for every induced DTMC $\MC_{\eta}$, there exists a DTMC $\MC \models_U \IMC$, such that $\MC = \MC_{\eta}$.
\end{lemma}
In other words the set of induced DTMCs by stationary natures on $\IMC$ is identical to the set of DTMCs that satisfy $\IMC$ under the UMC semantic.

\begin{proof}
    First notice, that $S^{\MC_{\eta}} = S^\IMC$ by definition, see \Cref{def:induced_DTMC_stat_Nature}.
\end{proof}

As stationary natures are a specific type of nature, \Cref{th:relation_of_semantics} follows directly from \Cref{lem:relation_stat_nature_UMC}.

\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}\label{th:two_DTMCs_compatible_for_max_reach}
    Given an IDTMC $\IMC$, two transient states $t_1, t_2\in S^\IMC$, a (goal) state $s\in S^\IMC$, and two DTMCs $\MC_1,\MC_2$ with $\MC_1,\MC_2\models_U \IMC$ and $Pr^{\MC_1}_{t_1}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^\IMC_{t_1}(\lozenge s)$ and $Pr^{\MC_2}_{t_2}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^{\IMC}_{t_2}(\lozenge s)$. In other words the DTMCs have a maximum reachability probability for $t_1$ and $t_2$, respectively.

    For all $u\in I^{\MC_1}_{t_1,s} \cap I^{\MC_2}_{t_2,s}$, the reachability probabilities $Pr^{\MC_1}_u(\lozenge s) = Pr^{\MC_2}_u(\lozenge s)$ are identical.
\end{corollary}


\begin{proof}
    This corollary follows from \Cref{th:max_reach_prob_for_intermediate_states}.
    Using \Cref{th:max_reach_prob_for_intermediate_states}, we can conclude that $Pr^{\MC_1}_{u}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^{\IMC}_{u}(\lozenge s)$ for all $u \in I^{\MC_1}_{t_1,s}$ and $Pr^{\MC_2}_{u}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^{\IMC}_{u}(\lozenge s)$ for all $u \in I^{\MC_2}_{t_2,s}$. Thus, $Pr^{\MC_1}_u(\lozenge s) = Pr^{\MC_2}_u(\lozenge s)$ for all $u\in I^{\MC_1}_{t_1,s} \cap I^{\MC_2}_{t_2,s}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-state Extension]\label{lem:one-state_extension}
    Given a set $T \subset S$, a state $t \notin T$, and a (goal) state s, assume that $\MC_T \models_U \IMC$ with $Pr_u^{\MC_T}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper_u^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ for all $u\in T$ and $\MC_t \models_U \IMC$ with $Pr_t^{\MC_t}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper_t^\IMC(\lozenge s)$. Define $\MC_{cmb}\models_U \IMC$ by
    \[\PF^{\MC_{cmb}}(w,v) = 
    \begin{cases*}
        \PF^{\MC_t}(w,v) & if $w\in I^{\MC_t}_{t,s}\backslash I^{\MC_T}_{T,s}$ \\
        \PF^{\MC_T}(w,v) & else.
    \end{cases*}\]
    Stated in words, $\MC_{cmb}$ is constructed such that its probability distribution function is equal to that of $\MC_t$ in all states that are only intermediate states for $t$ and not $T$, while it is equal to that of $\MC_T$ in all other states.
    
    Then, $Pr^{\MC_{cmb}}_u(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^{\MC_{cmb}}_{u}(\lozenge s)$ for all $u \in T \cup \{t\}$, i.e., $\MC_{cmb}$ has the maximum reachability probability for all states in $T\cup \{t\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    As $\PF^{\MC_{cmb}}(w,v) = \PF^{\MC_{T}}(w,v)$ for all $w \in I^{\MC_T}_{T,s}$, clearly $\Prupper^{\IMC}_{u}(\lozenge s) = Pr^{\MC_{cmb}}_u(\lozenge s)$ for all $u\in T$, see \Cref{lem:recursive_formula_for_reachability_prob}.

    Thus, we need to show that $Pr^{\MC_{cmb}}_t(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^{\IMC}_{t}(\lozenge s)$. As we know that $Pr^{\MC_{t}}_t(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^{\IMC}_{t}(\lozenge s)$, we will prove that $Pr^{\MC_{cmb}}_t(\lozenge s) = Pr^{\MC_{t}}_t(\lozenge s)$.
    Notice, that if $I^{\MC_t}_{t,s} \cap I^{\MC_T}_{T,s} = \emptyset$, then the proof concludes using the same argument as for $u\in T$. Furthermore, if $I^{\MC_t}_{t,s}\subseteq I^{\MC_T}_{T,s}$, then the proof concludes directly using \Cref{th:max_reach_prob_for_intermediate_states}.

    Recall, that $Pr^{\MC_{cmb}}_{t}(\lozenge s) = \sum_{u\in N(t)\cap I^{\MC_{cmb}}_{t,s}}\PF^{\MC_{cmb}}(t,u)\cdot Pr_u^{\MC_{cmb}}(\lozenge s)$, see \Cref{lem:recursive_formula_for_reachability_prob}. For all $u\in I^{\MC_t}_{t,s}\backslash I^{\MC_T}_{T,s}$, $\PF^{\MC_{cmb}}(u,w) = \PF^{\MC_{t}}(u,w)$ and for all $u\in I^{\MC_T}_{T,s} \cap I^{\MC_t}_{t,s}$, $Pr^{\MC_{cmb}}_u(\lozenge s) = Pr^{\MC_t}_u(\lozenge s)$, see \Cref{th:two_DTMCs_compatible_for_max_reach}. From this, we can conclude that $Pr^{\MC_{t}}_{t}(\lozenge s) = Pr^{\MC_{cmb}}_{t}(\lozenge s)$.
    Thus, we have shown that $Pr_u^{\MC_{cmb}}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper_u^{\MC_{cmb}}(\lozenge s)$ for all $u\in T \cup \{t\}$.

    Into only remains to show, that $\MC_{cmb} \models_U \IMC$. As $\MC_t,\MC_T \models_U \IMC$ it clearly follows from the construction that also $\MC_{cmb} \models \IMC$.
\end{proof}

Crucially, \Cref{lem:one-state_extension} can be extended to all transient states.

\begin{theorem}\label{th:existence_of_dtmc_with_universal_max_reach}
    Given an IDTMC $\IMC$ and a (goal) state $s\in S$ there exists a DTMC $\MC \models_U \IMC$ such that $Pr_t^\MC(\lozenge s) = \operatorname{sup}_{\MC_i \models_U \IMC} Pr_t^{\MC_i}(\lozenge s)$ for all transient states $t\in S^\IMC_{tr}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Consider any given enumeration $t_1, t_2, \dots$ of $S^\IMC$. Clearly, this enumeration is finite, since $S^\IMC$ is finite, see \Cref{assmp:finiteness_(I)DTMCs}.
    
    \emph{Construction.} Now, let $\MC^{(1)}\models_U \IMC$ with $Pr_{t_1}^{\MC^{(1)}}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^\IMC_{t_1}$ and $T_1 \coloneq I^{\MC^{(1)}}_{t_1,s}$, trivially such a DTMC must exist. By \Cref{th:max_reach_prob_for_intermediate_states}, $Pr_u(\lozenge s) = \PFupper_u(\lozenge s)$.

    Inductively, $\MC^{(n)} \models_U \IMC$ (and $s\in T_n$).
    If $t_{n+1}\in T_n$, then we set $\MC^{(n+1)} \coloneq \MC^{(n)}$ and $T_{n+1} = T_n$. If not, we use \Cref{lem:one-state_extension} with $T = T_n$, $\MC_T = \MC^{(n)}$, and $t = t_{n+1}$ to obtain $\MC^{(n+1)} \coloneq \MC_{cmb}$ with $\MC^{(n+1)}\models_U \IMC$. We set $T_{n+1} = T_n \cup I^{\MC^{(n+1)}}_{t_{n+1},s}$, thus and $Pr_{t_i}^{\MC^{(n+1)}}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper_{t_i}^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ for all $t_i \in T_{n+1}$, as $Pr_{t_i}^{\MC^{(n+1)}}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper_{t_i}^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ for all $t_i \in T_n$ (induction), $Pr_{t_{n+1}}^{\MC^{(n+1)}}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper_{t_{n+1}}^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ (see \Cref{lem:one-state_extension}), and $Pr_{u}^{\MC^{(n+1)}}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper_{u}^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ for all $u\in I^{\MC^{(n+1)}}_{t_{n+1},s}$ (see \Cref{th:max_reach_prob_for_intermediate_states}).
\end{proof}
Thus, to conclude, for any given IDTMC $\IMC$ and state $s$, there exists a $\MC$ with optimal reachability probability for all states $t\in S^\IMC$

\section{Calculating Expected Visiting Times of IDTMCs}
We will now have a look at how the \textit{expected visiting times} for IDTMCs can be calculated.

The most intuitive upper and lower bounds for the uEVTs and lEVTs of IDTMCs can be derived by using \Cref{eq:EVT_DTMC_iterative_characterization} and the upper and lower transition probability functions and initial distributions defined in \Cref{def:IMC}.

\begin{theorem}[Upper and Lower Bounds for uEVTs and lEVTs]\label{th:loose_bounds_for_evts}
    For any IDTMC $\IMC$, the uEVT is bounded from above by $\ExpUpper[vt_s] \leq \Exp_{upp}[vt_s] = \Initupper(s)+\sum_{t\in S_{tr}}\PFupper(t,s)^\T\cdot \Exp_{upp}[vt_t]$ and the $\ell$EVT is bounded from below by $\ExpLower[vt_s] \geq \Exp_{low}[vt_s] = \Initlower(s)+\sum_{t\in S_{tr}}\PFlower(t,s)^\T\cdot \Exp_{low}[vt_t]$ for all $s\in S_{tr}^\IMC$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    For any IDTMC $\IMC$ there exists a DTMC $\MC$ that satisfies $\IMC$, i.e., $\MC \models \IMC$, and $\Exp^\MC[vt_s] = \ExpUpper^\IMC[vt_s]$. As $\MC \models \IMC$, it follows that $\PF(s,s')\leq \PFupper(s,s')$ for all $s,s'\in S$.

    Thus, $\Exp^\MC[vt_s] = \iota_{init}(s)+\sum_{t\in S_{tr}}\PF(t,s)^\T\cdot \Exp[vt_t] \leq \Initupper(s)+\sum_{t\in S_{tr}}\PFupper(t,s)^\T\cdot \Exp_{upp}[vt_t] = \Exp_{upp}^\IMC[vt_s]$.
    
    The identical argument can be made to prove the lower bound for the $\ell$EVT.
\end{proof}

\subsection{UMC semantic}
In this section we examine how the precise values of the uEVTs and lEVTs can be calculated for IDTMCs under the UMC semantic.

As defined, there can be multiple DTMCs $\MC_i$ that satisfy an IDTMC $\IMC$ with differing transition probability functions $\PF^{\MC_i}$ and initial distributions $\iota_{init}^{\MC_i}$. To determine the uEVT and $\ell$EVT for a given state $s\in S^\IMC$, we thus have to find the satisfying DTMCs that maximize and minimze \Cref{eq:EVT_DTMC_iterative_characterization}, respectively.
In other words, we need to solve the equations
\[\Exp[vt_s] = \operatorname{opt}_{\MC\models\IMC} \left(\iota_{init}(s) + \sum_{t\in S_{tr}}\PF^\MC(t,s)\cdot\Exp[vt_t]\right), \qquad \operatorname{opt}\in\{\min,\max\}. \]

% As we concluded, the $\ell$EVT and uEVT of a given state $s$ in an IDTMC, can be calculated by computing the reachability probabilities $\Prupper^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $\Prupper^\IMC(\cCircle \lozenge s)$ or $\Prlower^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $\Prlower^\IMC(\cCircle \lozenge s)$. 
In \cite{complexity_model_checking_interval-valued_DTMCs}, the authors construct the following NLP (which we have rewritten slightly) to compute any maximum or minimum reachability, respectively, for a set of nodes $T\subseteq S$, using the fixed-initial-state-definition.

\[
   \begin{aligned}
        \operatorname{opt} \quad & \sum_{s\in S\backslash T}x_s & & \operatorname{opt}\in\{\max,\min\}\\
        \text{s.t.} \quad & x_s \sim \sum_{s'\in S\backslash T} \PF(s,s')x_{s'} + \sum_{s'\in T}\PF(s,s') && \forall s \in S\backslash T \text{ and } \sim \in \{\leq,\geq\}& (1)\\
        & x_s = 1 && \forall s \in T & (2)\\
        & \sum_{s_j\in N(s)} \PF(s_i,s_j) = 1 && \forall s_i\in S_{tr} & (3)\\
        & \PFlower^\IMC(s_i,s_j) \leq \PF(s_i,s_j) \leq \PFupper^\IMC(s_i,s_j) && \forall s_i,s_j\in S_{tr}^\IMC & (4)\\
    \end{aligned}
\]
(Allowed only max + $\leq$ and min + $\geq$)

\todo{alternatively can be calculated over LP or VI for max./min. reachability probabilities in MDPs for memoryless schedulers; gezeigt in der Katoen-Bibel}

As shown in \cite{complexity_model_checking_interval-valued_DTMCs}, this NLP can be solved in PTIME. Thus, we can calculate the maximum or minimum reachability probabilities $\Prupper^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $\Prupper^\IMC(\cCircle \lozenge s)$ or $\Prlower^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $\Prlower^\IMC(\cCircle \lozenge s)$ of a state $s$ in polynomial time. 
As we showed in \Cref{th:existence_of_dtmc_with_universal_max_reach} in \Cref{sec:IMC_Quantitative_Reachability}, there exist a DTMCs $\MC_{max}, \MC_{min} \models_U \IMC$ for any given IDTMC $\IMC$ that have maximum or minimum reachability from all states to $s$, i.e., $Pr^{\MC_{max}}_t(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^\IMC_t(\lozenge s)$  or $Pr^{\MC_{min}}_t(\lozenge s) = \Prlower^\IMC_t(\lozenge s)$ for all $t\in S^\IMC$. Thus, clearly also $Pr^{\MC_{max}}(\lozenge s) = \Prupper^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $Pr^{\MC_{max}}(\cCircle\lozenge s) = \Prupper^\IMC(\cCircle \lozenge s)$ or $Pr^{\MC_{min}}(\lozenge s) = \Prlower^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $Pr^{\MC_{min}}(\cCircle\lozenge s) = \Prlower^\IMC(\cCircle \lozenge s)$. Using \Cref{eq:EVT_via_quant_reach_sum}, we can then calculate $\Exp^{\MC_{max}}[vt_s]$ or $\Exp^{\MC_{min}}[vt_s]$.

\begin{theorem}
    Given $\MC_{max}\models \IMC$, there exists no $\MC \models \IMC$ such that $\Exp^{\MC_{max}}[vt_s] < \Exp^{\MC}[vt_s]$.
    Conversely, given $\MC_{min}$, there exists no $\MC \models \IMC$ such that $\Exp^{\MC_{min}}[vt_s] > \Exp^{\MC}[vt_s]$.
    In other words $\Exp^{\MC_{max}}[vt_s] = \ExpUpper^\IMC[vt_s]$ and $\Exp^{\MC_{min}}[vt_s] = \ExpLower^\IMC[vt_s]$.
\end{theorem}

We will prove this theorem by contradiction.
\begin{proof}
    Let us assume that there exists a DTMC $\MC \models \IMC$ such that $\Exp^{\MC_{max}}[vt_s] < \Exp^{\MC}[vt_s]$. Using \Cref{eq:EVT_via_quant_reach_sum}, it thus follows that either $Pr^\MC(\Diamond s) > Pr^{\MC_{max}}(\Diamond s)$ or $Pr^\MC(\cCircle\Diamond s) > Pr^{\MC_{max}}(\cCircle\Diamond s)$. However, as $Pr^{\MC_{max}}(\Diamond s) = \Prupper^{\IMC}(\Diamond s)$ and $Pr^{\MC_{max}}(\cCircle\Diamond s) = \Prupper^ {\IMC}(\cCircle\Diamond s)$ this leads to a contradiciton.
    The proof for $\MC_{min}$ follows analogously by reversing the inequalities.
\end{proof}

In other words, we can calculate the $\ell$EVT and uEVT of a state $s$ in a given IDTMC under the UMC semantic by determining the maximum reachability probabilities $\Prupper^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $\Prupper^\IMC(\cCircle\lozenge s)$ or the minimum reachability probabilities $\Prlower^\IMC(\lozenge s)$ and $\Prlower^\IMC(\cCircle\lozenge s)$.

As stated in \Cref{th:k-step_transition_probability} the $k$-th power of the transition matrik $\tT^k$ contains the probabilities of transitioning from any state $i$ to any other state $j$ in precisely $k$ steps. Thus, the product $\iota_{init}^\T \cdot \tT^k$ characterizes the probability distribution of the current state of the given DTMC in step $k$. We define this product as $\theta_k^\T = \iota_{init}^\T \cdot \tT^k$, where the $i$-th entry of $\theta_k$ characterizes the probability that the current state in step $k$ is $i$. We can also define $\theta_k$ iteratively through $\theta_k^\T = \theta_{k-1}^\T \cdot \tT$

\begin{definition}[Stationary Distributions in DTMCs]
    A distribution $\theta_k^\MC$ in a DTMC $\MC$ is stationary if and only if $\theta_k^\T = \theta_k^\T \cdot \tT^\MC$, i.e. the probability distribution of the current state remains unchanged through future steps. We denote the stationary distribution as $\theta^\MC$, where $\theta^\MC(s)$ denotes the entry of $\theta^\MC$ which corresponds to state $s$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Stationary Distributions in IDTMCs]
    For a given IDTMC $\IMC$, we define $\Theta = \{\theta^\MC \mid \MC \models \IMC\}$ as the set of stationary distributions. Furthermore, for a given state $s\in S^\IMC$, we define $\Thetalower^\IMC(s)$ and $\Thetaupper^\IMC(s)$ such that $\Thetalower^\IMC(s) = \operatorname{inf}_{\MC\models\IMC}\theta^\MC(s)$ and $\Thetaupper^\IMC(s) = \operatorname{sup}_{\MC\models\IMC}\theta^\MC(s)$.
\end{definition}
