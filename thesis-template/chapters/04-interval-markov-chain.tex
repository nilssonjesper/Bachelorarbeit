We start by introducing concepts which we will use in the following sections.
\begin{definition}[Interval Markov Chain]\label{def:IMC}
    An interval discrete-time Markov chain (IDTMC) is a tuple
    $\IMC=(S^\IMC,\PFlower^\IMC,\PFupper^\IMC,\Initlower^\IMC,\Initupper^\IMC)$, where
    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
        \item $S^\IMC$ is a countable, nonempty set of states,
        \item $\PFlower^\IMC: S^\IMC \times S^\IMC \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the lower bound on the transition probability function,
        \item $\PFupper^\IMC: S^\IMC \times S^\IMC \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the upper bound on the transition probability function,
        \item $\Initlower^\IMC: S^\IMC \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the lower bound on the initial distribution, and
        \item $\Initupper^\IMC: S^\IMC \rightarrow [0,1]$ is the upper bound on the initial distribution,
    \end{enumerate}
    such that $\PFlower^\IMC(s_i,s_j) \leq \PFupper^\IMC(s_i,s_j)$ for all $s_i,s_j\in S^\IMC$ and $\Initlower^\IMC(s) \leq \Initupper^\IMC(s)$ for all $s\in S^\IMC$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Path of an IDTMC]
    Similarly to DTMCs, an \textit{infinite path} in an IDTMC $\IMC = (S,)$ is a sequence $\pi = (s_0,s_1,\dots)$ of states with $s_0,s_1,\dots \in S^\IMC$, such that $\PFlower(\pi[k],\pi[k+1])>0$ for all $k\in \NN$.
\end{definition}
Furthermore, we define the length $|\pi|$ of a finite path $\pi = (s_0,\dots,s_k) \in Paths_{fin}^\IMC$ to be equal to $k$, which we denote as $|\pi| = k$. As an infinite path has infinite length, we define $\pi \in Paths_{inf}^\IMC$ to be $|\pi| = \infty$. Moreover, we denote the last state of a finite path $\pi \in Paths^{\IMC}_{fin}$ as $last(\pi)$, i.e., if $|\pi| = n$, then $last(\pi) = \pi[n]$.

\emph{\textbf{Fixed-initial-state definition.}}
In the literature, see for example \cite{complexity_model_checking_interval-valued_DTMCs,Model-Checking_presence_Uncertainties,Semantics_Quant-Analysis}, it is common to represent the initial distribution by an additional initial state rather than introducing a separate initial-distribution function. In this case, an IMC is given as a tuple $\IMC_{\mathrm{fix}} = (S,\PFlower,\PFupper,s_{\mathrm{in}}),$ where \(s_{\mathrm{in}}\) is the unique initial state. We refer to this as the \emph{fixed-initial-state definition}.

More precisely, let $\IMC = (S^\IMC,\PFlower^\IMC,\PFupper^\IMC,\Initlower^\IMC,\Initupper^\IMC)$ be an IMC in our definition. The corresponding \textit{fixed-initial-state} representation is obtained by introducing a fresh state \(s_{\mathrm{in}}\) and defining $S^{\IMC_{\mathrm{fix}}} = S^\IMC\cup\{s_{\mathrm{in}}\}.$

The lower and upper transition probability functions are given by
\[
\PFlower^{\IMC_{\mathrm{fix}}}(s_i,s_j)
=
\begin{cases*}
    \PFlower^\IMC(s_i,s_j)
        & if $s_i,s_j\neq s_{\mathrm{in}}$,\\
    \Initlower^\IMC(s_j)
        & if $s_i=s_{\mathrm{in}}$,\\
    0 & otherwise
\end{cases*}
\]
and
\[
\PFupper^{\IMC_{\mathrm{fix}}}(s_i,s_j)
=
\begin{cases*}
    \PFupper^\IMC(s_i,s_j)
        & if $s_i,s_j\neq s_{\mathrm{in}}$,\\
    \Initupper^\IMC(s_j)
        & if $s_i=s_{\mathrm{in}}$,\\
    0 & otherwise.
\end{cases*}
\]
The same construction applies to DTMCs. Let $\MC=(S^\MC,\PF^\MC,\iota^\MC_{\mathrm{init}})$ be a DTMC. Its corresponding \textit{fixed-initial-state} representation is $\MC_{\mathrm{fix}} = (S^\MC\cup\{s_{\mathrm{in}}\},\PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}},s_{\mathrm{in}}),$ where
\[
    \PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}}(s_i,s_j)
    =
    \begin{cases*}
        \PF^\MC(s_i,s_j)
            & if $s_i,s_j\neq s_{\mathrm{in}}$,\\
        \iota^\MC_{\mathrm{init}}(s_j)
            & if $s_i=s_{\mathrm{in}}$,\\
        0 & otherwise.
    \end{cases*}
\]
The two representations induce the same probability measure under the natural bijection between their paths. In particular, we define
\[
    \phi: Paths_{\inf}^{\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}} \longrightarrow Paths_{\inf}^{\MC}, (s_{\mathrm{in}},s_0,s_1,\ldots) \mapsto (s_0,s_1,\ldots)\ .
\]

Notice, that $\phi$ is a bijection, since $s_{\mathrm{in}}$ has no incoming transitions, every infinite path of $\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}$ starts with $s_{\mathrm{in}}$.

To see that the probability measures correspond under $\phi$, consider a finite path $\pi_{\mathrm{fix}} = (s_{\mathrm{in}},s_0,\ldots,s_n)$ of $\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}$, and let $\pi=(s_0,\ldots,s_n)$ be the corresponding finite path of $\MC$. Then
\begin{align}
Pr^{\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}}(\pi_{\mathrm{fix}}) 
&= \PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}}(s_{\mathrm{in}},s_0) \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \PF^{\MC_{\mathrm{fix}}}(s_k,s_{k+1}) \notag
&= \iota^\MC_{\mathrm{init}}(s_0) \prod_{k=0}^{n-1} \PF^\MC(s_k,s_{k+1}) \notag
&= Pr^\MC(\pi).
\end{align}
Thus, corresponding cylinder sets have the same probability, and consequently the probability measures on the two path spaces coincide under the bijection $\phi$.

Hence, adding a unique initial state is merely a different representation of the initial distribution and does not change the probabilistic behaviour of the model. In particular, results formulated for the \textit{fixed-initial-state definition} can be transferred to our definition by applying the above construction.

To resolve the uncertainty in the initial distribution and transition probability function, we need to define semantics that specify a fixed initial distribution and transition probability function. In other words, the semantics specify a set of Markov Chains that are consistent with the given Interval Markov Chain. We say that all of these DTMCs $\MC$ satisfy the IDTMC $\IMC$ and denote it as $\MC \models \IMC$. We assume in this thesis that there exists at least one satisfying DTMC for every IDTMC.

\begin{assumption}\label{assmp:finiteness_IDTMCs}
    In this thesis, we solely consider finite IDTMCs $\IMC$, i.e., $S^\IMC$ is finite. We sometimes refer to these finite IDTMCs, as \textit{finite-state IDTMCs}.
    Moreover, we restrict our consideration to IDTMCs for which, for every state $s\in S^\IMC$, there exists at least one satisfying DTMC $\MC \models \IMC$, such that $s$ is reachable with a positive probability from an initial state in $\MC$, i.e., $Pr^\MC(\lozenge s) > 0$. If a given state were unreachable in every satisfying DTMC, then its EVT would trivially be $0$ for all satisfying DTMCs and, consequently, could not contribute to the EVTs of other states.
\end{assumption}

In this thesis, we consider two popular semantics: the \textit{once-and-for-all semantic}, also known as the \textit{Uncertain Markov Chain (UMC) semantic}, and the \textit{IMDP semantic}.
We will first define the former, similar to \cite{Semantics_Quant-Analysis,Semantics_Model-Checking_omega-Regular,Semantics_LTL_Model_Checking}. To this end, we fix an IDTMC $\IMC = (S^\IMC,\PFlower,\PFupper,\Initlower,\Initupper)$ and a DTMC $\MC = (S^\MC,\PF,\iota_{init})$ for the rest of this chapter.

\begin{definition}[Once-and-for-all/UMC Semantic]\label{def:once-and-for-all_semantic}
    A DTMC $\MC$ satisfies an IDTMC $\IMC$ under the UMC semantic if and only if $S^\IMC = S^\MC$ and
    \[\PF^{\MC}(s_i,s_j)\in[\PFlower^{\IMC}(s_i,s_j),\PFupper^{\IMC}(s_i,s_j)] \qquad \text{and} \qquad \iota_{init}^{\MC}(s)\in[\Initlower^{\IMC}(s),\Initupper^{\IMC}(s)]\]
    for all $s_i,s_j,s\in S^\IMC$.
\end{definition}
In other words, any DTMC whose initial distribution and transition probabilities lie in the intervals defined by the IDTMC, is satisfactory. Thus, we can understand the UMC semantic for IDTMCs, as choosing the initial distribution and probability function from the intervals \textit{once-and-for-all}, hence its name.
% Moreover, notice that $\MC$ has the same cardinality as $\IMC$, i.e., $|S^\MC| = |S^\IMC|$.
If $\MC$ satisfies $\IMC$ under the UMC semantic, we denote this as $\MC\models_{\text{U}}\IMC$.
As $\MC\models_{\mathrm{U}}\IMC$ implies $S^\MC=S^\IMC$, we use the same symbol $s\in S^\MC=S^\IMC$ to denote the corresponding state in both $\MC$ and $\IMC$.

\end{example}

While the \textit{UMC} semantic selects a particular probability function \textit{once-and-for-all}, the \textit{IMDP} semantic allows for a probability function to be picked from the set of valid functions before every transition. \todo{"valid" functions?; gute Formulierung?}
Similar to \cite{Model-Checking_presence_Uncertainties} and \cite{Semantics_Model-Checking_omega-Regular}, we define the set of valid probability functions over $S^\IMC$ as $Steps^\IMC(s)$, i.e., 
\[Steps^\IMC(s) = \{\mu \mid \sum_{t\in S^\IMC} \mu(t) = 1 \text{ and } \PFlower(s,t) \leq \mu(t) \leq \PFupper(s,t) \text{ for all } t\in S^\IMC\}\]
for all $s\in S^\IMC$.
In other words, under the \textit{IMDP} semantic, any probability function from $Steps^\IMC(s)$ can be chosen before each transition. \todo{vllt zu stark eine Doppelung des vorherigen Satzes?; \& ist "entered" ein passendes wort?}
Furthermore, $Steps^\IMC = \bigcup_{s\in S^\IMC} Steps^\IMC(s)$ and $\mu(s,t)$ denotes the probability that the probability distribution $\mu(s)$ assigns to $t$, for $s,t\in S^\IMC$.
This choice of a probability function is made non-deterministically by an external environment.

In \cite{Badings2025} this external environment is defined as the \textit{nature}.
\begin{definition}[Nature]\label{def:nature}
    A \textit{nature} for an IDTMC $\IMC$ is a function $\eta: Paths^\IMC_{fin} \rightarrow Steps^\IMC$ that maps each finite path on to a valid transition probability function, i.e., $\eta(\pi)\in Steps^\IMC(last(\pi))$.
\end{definition}
Intuitively, this implies that $\eta$, i.e., the external environment, depends on all states that the model has visited up until $last(\pi)$ for any given $\pi \in Paths_{fin}^\IMC$.
Notice, that we only define this external environment for the transition probability function and not the initial distribution. Recall, that results for the \textit{fixed-initial-state definition} can be applied to our definition. Thus, it suffices to define this external environment for the transition probability function. For the rest of this chapter, we fix a nature $\eta: Paths^\IMC_{fin} \rightarrow Steps^\IMC$.

By applying a given \textit{nature} $\eta$ to $\IMC$, we resolve the uncertainty of the probability function, as $\eta$ specifies a probability function $\PF \in Steps^\IMC(s)$ for every finite path in $\IMC$.
Thus, $\eta$ induces a DTMC as follows.

\begin{definition}[DTMC of an IDTMC Induced by a Nature]\label{def:DTMC_induced_by_nature}
    For an IDTMC $\IMC$ and a nature $\eta$ on $\IMC$, the induced Markov Chain $\MC_{\eta}$ is given by
    \[\MC_{\eta} = (Paths^\IMC_{fin},\PF^{\MC_{\eta}},s_{in})\ ,\]
    where for all $\pi \in Paths_{fin}^\IMC$, $s\in S^\IMC$, and $\eta(\pi) = \mu(last(\pi))$: 
    \[\PF^{\MC_{\eta}}(\pi,\pi') = \begin{cases*}
        \mu(last(\pi),s) & if $\pi' = (\pi[0],\pi[1],\dots,last(\pi),s)$ \\
        0 & otherwise.
    \end{cases*}
    \]
\end{definition}
This definition is based on \cite{Bibel}.

Notice, that these induced DTMCs are infinite in size, i.e., $|\MC_{\eta}| = \infty$ and that, intuitively, every state $s\in S^{\MC_{\eta}}$ is visited only once, as the states represent paths. Thus, $\MC_\eta$ is an acyclic DTMC. 

To relate the states in the induced DTMCs $\MC_{\eta}$ to the states in the IDTMC $\IMC$, we define the function
\[ \tau: Paths_{fin}^\IMC \rightarrow S^\IMC,\ (\pi[0],\dots,last(\pi)) \mapsto last(\pi)\ .\]
In other words, the states in the induced DTMCs that correspond to paths in $\IMC$ ending in some state $s\in S^\IMC$, are mapped to $s$. Intuitively, 
\[\tau^{-1}(s) = \{\pi \in Paths_{fin}^\IMC \mid \tau(\pi) = s\}\]
defines the set of all states in the induced DTMCs that correspond to paths in $\IMC$ ending in $s$ for all $s\in S^\IMC$. Moreover, we denote $\tau^{-1}(T) = \bigcup_{t\in T}\tau^{-1}(t)$ for $T\subseteq S^\IMC$.\todo{vllt brauche ich dieses set nicht}

Of particular relevance to this thesis are \textit{stationary natures}. The following definition is based on \cite{Badings2025}.

\begin{definition}[Stationary Nature]\label{def:stat_nature}
    A nature $\eta$ for an IDTMC $\IMC$ is \textit{stationary} if for all paths $\pi,\pi' \in Paths_{fin}^\IMC$, it holds that 
    \[ last(\pi) = last(\pi') \implies \eta(\pi) = \eta(\pi')\ .\]
\end{definition}
Intuitively, this implies that $\eta$ only depends on the current state of the model, i.e., the state the model is in. Thus, stationary natures are functions of the form 
\[\eta: S^\IMC \rightarrow Steps^\IMC \ .\]

    \[ \PF^{\MC_\eta}(s_i,s_j) = \mu(s_i,s_j) \]

First, we introduce reachability probabilities for IDTMCs. We base our definition of the quantitative reachability property in IDTMCs on \cite{reachability_in_pIMCs_using_constraints}. 
\begin{definition}[Reachability Probability in IDTMCs]\label{def:reachability_probability_IDTMCs}
    Given an IDTMC $\IMC$, a set of states $T \subseteq S^\IMC$, $p \in [0,1]$, and $\sim \in \{\leq,\geq\}$, then a quantitative reachability property on $\IMC$ under the UMC semantic is defined as 
    $Pr^\IMC(\lozenge T) \sim p$, if and only if there exists at least one DTMC $\MC \models_U \IMC$ such that $Pr^\MC(\lozenge T) \sim p$. 
    Further, we define the quantitative reachability property on $\IMC$ under the IMDP semantic as 
    $Pr^\IMC(\lozenge T) \sim p$, if and only if there exists at least one DTMC $\MC \models_I \IMC$ such that $Pr^\MC(\bigcup_{t\in\rho(T)}\lozenge t) \sim p$ under the IMDP semantic.
\end{definition}

In this thesis, we are particularly interested in the optimal reachability probabilities, which we define as follows.

\begin{definition}[Maxmimum and Minimum Reachability Probability]
    Given an IDTMC $\IMC$ and a set of states $T\subseteq S$, we define the maximum reachability as $\sup_{\MC\models_U \IMC}Pr^{\MC}(\lozenge T)$ and the minimum reachability as $\inf_{\MC\models_U \IMC}Pr^{\MC}(\lozenge T)$ under the UMC semantic.

    Furthermore, we define the maximum reachability as $\sup_{\MC\models_I \IMC}Pr^\MC(\bigcup_{t\in\rho(T)}\lozenge t)$ and the minimum reachability as $\inf_{\MC\models_I \IMC}Pr^\MC(\bigcup_{t\in\rho(T)}\lozenge t)$ under the IMDP semantic.

\end{definition}
To shorten the notation, we denote the maximum reachability as $\Prupper^\MC(\lozenge s)$ and the minimum reachability as $\Prlower^\MC(\lozenge s)$.
Moreover, we use the notation $\Prupper^\IMC_s(\lozenge T)$ and $\Prlower^\IMC_s(\lozenge T)$ to denote the maximum and minimum reachability probabilities, when fixing $s\in S^\IMC$ as the unique initial state.

Furthermore, we define transient and recurrent states. As we observed for the infinite-state DTMCs $\MC_\eta$ defined in \Cref{def:DTMC_induced_by_nature}, every state $s\in S^{\MC_\eta}$ is visited only once. Thus, intuitively $Pr_s(\cCircle\lozenge s) = 0$ for all $s\in S^{\MC_\eta}$, meaning $S^{\MC_\eta}$ contains no recurrent states. Consequently, we define transient and recurrent states exclusively for the UMC semantic.
\begin{definition}[Transient and Recurrent states in IDTMCs]\label{def:transient_recurrent_states_IDTMC}
    Given an IDTMC $\IMC$, a state $s\in S^\IMC$ is called recurrent if and only if $\Prupper^\IMC_s(\cCircle\lozenge s) = 1$, i.e., there exists at least one satisfying DTMC $\MC\models_U\IMC$ such that $Pr^\MC_s(\cCircle\lozenge s) = 1$. 
    
    Furthermore, a state $s\in S^\IMC$ is called transient if and only if $\Prlower^\IMC_s(\cCircle\lozenge s) < 1$, i.e., there exists at least one satisfying DTMC $\MC\models_U\IMC$ such that $Pr^\MC_s(\cCircle\lozenge s) < 1$.

\end{definition}

\section{Relation between the semantics}\label{sec:relation_between_the_semantics}
In this section, we show some relations between the UMC and IMDP semantic that are relevant for the rest of this thesis.

Intuitively, the \textit{IMDP} semantic is more expressive than the \textit{UMC} semantic. More specifically for any given IDTMC $\IMC$, a DTMC $\MC$ that satisfies $\IMC$ according to the UMC semantic, i.e., $\MC \models_U \IMC$, does also satisfy $\IMC$ according to the IMDP semantic, i.e., $\MC \models_I \IMC$.
\begin{theorem}\label{th:relation_of_semantics}
    For any IDTMC $\IMC$ and DTMC $\MC$, if $\MC \models_U \IMC$, then $\MC \models_I \IMC$.
\end{theorem}

We shortly prove this theorem.

\begin{proof}
    Given an IDTMC $\IMC$ and a DTMC $\MC \models_U \IMC$, there exists at least one stationary nature $\eta_{st}$ and corresponding induced DTMC $\MC_{\eta_{st}}$ of the form \Cref{def:induced_DTMC_stat_Nature}, such that $\MC = \MC_{\eta_{st}}$.

    In other words the set of induced DTMCs by stationary natures on $\IMC$ of the form \Cref{def:induced_DTMC_stat_Nature} is identical to the set of DTMCs that satisfy $\IMC$ under the UMC semantic.

    To show this, we first define $\eta_{st}(s) \coloneq \PF^\MC(s)$ for all $s \in S^\IMC = S^\MC$, where $\PF^\MC(s_i)$ denotes the probability distribution of $\PF^\MC$ on $s_i$. Thus, 
    \[\PF^{\MC_{\eta_{st}}}(s_i,s_j) = \eta_{st}(s_i,s_j) = \PF^\MC(s_i,s_j)\]
    for all $s_i,s_j\in S^\IMC$, where $\mu(s_i) = \eta(s_i)$. In other words, $\MC_{\eta_{st}}$ and $\MC$ have the identical probability distribution function.
    Moreover, $S^{\MC_{\eta_{st}}} = S^\IMC = S^\MC$ by definition, see \Cref{def:induced_DTMC_stat_Nature} and \Cref{def:once-and-for-all_semantic}.
